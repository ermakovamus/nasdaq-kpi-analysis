02 KPI Assessment

📊
Completeness
What fraction of expected data is present?
Row Completeness = (Rows Collected / Expected Trading Days) × 100%
Cell Completeness = (1 − NaN ratio) × 100%
Overall = Average of both scores

Measures whether all expected rows (trading days) and all columns within those rows contain valid data. Missing rows may indicate API gaps or market holidays not excluded from the expected count.

Latency
How fresh is the data relative to today?
Calendar Lag = Collection Date − Last Data Point Date
Business Lag = count of business days in that gap
Rating: ≤1 biz day = Excellent | ≤3 = Good | ≤7 = Acceptable | else Poor

Latency measures the delay between the most recent available data point and the moment of collection. For trading datasets, 1 business day is the minimum possible lag (T+1 settlement basis).

Accuracy
Are the values logically correct?
For each row, check:
 • High ≥ Low
 • High ≥ Open AND High ≥ Close
 • Low ≤ Open AND Low ≤ Close
 • Volume ≥ 0
Accuracy % = (rows passing ALL checks / total rows) × 100

Without a ground-truth reference, accuracy is assessed via internal logical consistency of OHLCV values. A perfectly accurate financial dataset should have 100% of rows satisfying all constraints.

Consistency
Are values uniform and free of anomalies?
Start at 100. Deduct for:
 • Non-numeric columns: −20 pts
 • Duplicate dates: −5 pts each (max −20)
 • Volume outliers (>μ+3σ): proportional −20 max
 • |Daily return| > 20%: proportional −20 max

Consistency checks that all records follow the same schema, have no duplicate indices, and that statistical outliers do not indicate data corruption (extreme single-day volume spikes or price moves).

04 Conclusion

The NASDAQ stock price dataset collected via the Yahoo Finance API demonstrates high overall quality across all four evaluated KPI dimensions. Five companies were deliberately chosen with different time periods (ranging from 3 months for META to 12 months for AAPL) to introduce variability in dataset size and coverage, making the KPI comparison richer and more meaningful.

Completeness scores ranged from 97–99%, with minor gaps attributable to US market holidays that are correctly absent from the trading calendar rather than true missing data. The yfinance library already handles holiday exclusion, so these gaps are expected and acceptable.

Latency was uniformly Excellent (1 business day lag) for all five tickers, reflecting the fact that data was collected on April 26, 2025, and the most recent available data point was April 25, 2025 — the last full trading day. This is the theoretical minimum for end-of-day data feeds.

Accuracy scored 100% across all tickers. Every row in the collected dataset satisfies all six internal OHLCV logical constraints (High ≥ Low, price bounds, non-negative volume). This is consistent with Yahoo Finance's auto-adjustment mechanism which corrects for corporate actions like stock splits and dividends.

Consistency scored 95–98 across tickers (out of 100). The minor deductions stem from a small number of statistically extreme volume days (e.g., earnings announcement days for NVDA), which are not errors but genuine market events. All columns maintained correct numeric dtypes and no duplicate date indices were found.

Overall: This dataset is suitable for use as a training dataset for AI/ML financial models, provided users account for the different time period lengths and normalise or align the data as needed. The Yahoo Finance API offers an excellent balance of accessibility, timeliness, and data integrity for academic and research purposes.
